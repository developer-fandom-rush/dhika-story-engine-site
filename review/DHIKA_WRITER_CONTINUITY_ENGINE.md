Purpose: a future reviewer/writer should not have to reconstruct pacing, erotic motion, visual access, repetition, and relationship state by opening dozens of old Parts. RAW remains the highest authority; this file is the cumulative authoring map.

This file is descriptive. It does not slow the story down and does not invent future events.

# 3. Core progression axes

Future writing must track progression on multiple axes. Do not reduce progression to “what body part is revealed next.”

## A. Visual access
What has actually been seen, for how long, and whether the view was accidental, deliberate, repeated, caught, or mutually known.

## B. Mutual awareness
A look can escalate without any new body visibility:
- unseen look,
- look noticed,
- look caught,
- look verbally teased,
- denial,
- shared eye contact,
- both know the other knows.

## C. Intent
The same visual can have different progression value:
- accidental,
- unplanned but noticed,
- curiosity,
- deliberate test,
- deliberate tease,
- deliberate desire to be looked at.

## D. Duration / repetition
A repeated or sustained look can escalate more than a new one-second reveal.

## E. Proximity / touch
Track exact touch history separately from visual history.

## F. Internal state
Attraction, fear, excitement, guilt, curiosity, self-restraint, and rationalization can move the relationship even when no physical event occurs.

## G. Knowledge asymmetry
Many DHIKA scenes work because reader, Dhika, Chotu, John, Uncle, Bantu or Bobby know different things.

# 10. Repetition / frequency patterns

Future writers must track recurrence, not merely first occurrences.

## Compliments
Dhika receiving compliments is recurrent across Bantu, John, Chotu and others.
Her response varies by person/context.
Do not write every compliment as a shocking first.

## Chotu gaze
Not one isolated incident.
By E03 it is a repeated pattern:

## Dhika teasing
Also recurrent:
playful naming/cheek taps → verbal stomach tease → girlfriend/kiss teasing → later deliberate clothing tease → explicit wish that Chotu look.

## Accidental visibility
The story repeats accidental visibility, but the emotional meaning changes.
Do not assume repetition means bad pacing; author uses recurrence to change trust/knowledge.

## Aftermath beats
Large events are often followed by:
- private reflection,
- guilt,
- self-rationalization,
- temporary return to ordinary life,
- route-switch,
before next escalation.

This is rhythm, not a mandatory delay quota.

# 11. Author rhythm: what not to destroy

## Minimal explanation inside erotic prose
The source often works through:
clothes / posture → gaze → tiny reaction → tease → denial/silence → continuation.

Do not insert analytical paragraphs explaining every motive.

## Reader participation
Leave room for the reader to infer tension from:
- eyes,
- posture,
- pause,
- blush,
- lip bite,
- changed voice,
- missing habit,
- repeated glance.

## Ordinary logistics are engines
Food, deliveries, forgotten items, painting, shelves, dupatta, scooter, household tasks and study schedules repeatedly create proximity/visibility.

Do not force every charged scene to start from explicit erotic intention.

## Fast is allowed when micro-beats exist
DHIKA does not require “one body reveal per episode.”
It can move quickly if:
- prior state supports it,
- micro-beats connect it,
- reactions are preserved,
- knowledge/intent changes are visible.

# 12. Relationship History Ledger audit

Current pointer ledgers are useful for navigation but are not sufficient as a future-writing memory by themselves.

Why:
- pointers say where something happened, not what writer lesson must persist,
- they do not automatically preserve visual-access accumulation,
- they do not teach pacing/repetition,
- a future chat can read only the latest pointer list and still miss why the progression matters.

Therefore:
- pointer ledger = navigation,
- Relationship Progression = detailed current relationship state,
- this Writer Continuity Engine = cumulative authoring/pacing state.

All three serve different purposes.

# 13. Prediction method

Predict only the immediate next range/mechanism.

Before prediction:
- previous hard-stop,
- unresolved practical hooks,
- visual access already established,
- internal state,
- recurring habits,
- knowledge gaps.

Predict:
- likely mechanism/direction,
not exact invented choreography.

Then freeze prediction and compare to RAW.

A prediction miss is valuable if the actual introduces a new author pattern.

# 14. Failure modes that must not recur

1. Treating safety/QA language as a story-pacing instruction.
2. Assuming “not established yet” means “should happen much later.”
3. Resetting visual access.
4. Calling repeated gaze/exposure a first-time beat.
5. Ignoring duration.
6. Ignoring recurrence/frequency.
7. Explaining erotic subtext inside story prose instead of letting it play.
8. Replacing exact source intensity with abstract labels.
9. Reading only latest Part without E01/E02 cumulative history.
10. Following a pointer ledger without opening the relationship state it points to.
11. Inventing motives to make a scene “safer” or more justified.
12. Compressing a causal micro-beat chain into a generic summary.
